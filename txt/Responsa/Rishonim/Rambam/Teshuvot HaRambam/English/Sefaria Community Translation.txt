Teshuva 165

...Question 165
...Question: A pot of porridge is left on a stove over Shabbat. On the following day, when the pot is removed from the stove, a wooden stirring stick is inserted into it. The mixture is stirred vigorously and mixed well. It is then beaten with the wooden stick either against the sides of the pot or within a bowl, until the meat, fat, and liquid are blended together and become a single, uniform mass. Sometimes, water is removed from the top before mixing, and at other times, hot water is added, depending on what is needed. Are all these actions prohibited or permitted? 
Response: Certainly, all of these are permitted. For we find in a clear Tannaitic explanation regarding garlic, unripe grapes, and roasted grain kernels that were crushed before Shabbat that Rabbi Yishmael says one may finish grinding them after nightfall, while Rabbi Akiva says one may not finish grinding them. And it is established that this dispute applies only when further grinding is necessary, and the halachah is according to Rabbi Yishmael.
And the matter is even more lenient by logical inference: If roasted grain kernels, which still require grinding, may be ground on Shabbat, then certainly, how much more so this porridge mixture—which has already been ground and fully cooked and only lacks mixing—may be mixed!
The only action that would be prohibited is when the food still requires full pounding, as will be clearly explained there. When something requires pounding, all agree that it is forbidden. But this porridge does not require pounding, since it was already pounded sufficiently in the mortar and fully cooked. What remains is only a bit of mashing and mixing, which is permitted. Likewise, adding hot water to the pot is permitted.
Therefore, it is permitted to perform all the described actions after removing the pot from the stove, since the only activity involved is light grinding and mixing, which is allowed.
Moshe ben Maimon, may the memory of the righteous be for a blessing.
