But that is not the case—rather, he did it to evade (a direct response). "Is the matter too wondrous?" And would Rav Pappa utter a falsehood by saying something in the name of Rava that Rava never said at all? However, according to what was explained, it makes sense: Rava correctly stated that the halakha follows Beit Hillel. This applies when one brings (the oil) during the meal to remove filth, in which case one recites the blessing "Borei Shemen Arev" (Who creates pleasant oil). However, when one brings it not during the meal, even though it is not aged oil nor finely ground, and it is at the very least persimmon oil (which warrants the blessing "Borei Shemen Arev"), nevertheless, since it is also brought for smelling, the hadas (myrtle) takes precedence, for we have merited both its fragrance and its anointing. And this follows Rabban Gamliel, who ruled in this case in favor of Beit Shammai. Rava does not disagree with Rabban Gamliel at all, and the case of Rav Pappa occurred outside of a meal. Nevertheless, he first recited the blessing on the hadas, and Rav Huna said to him, "Does the master not hold that the halakha follows the words of the one who mediates (Rabban Gamliel)?" Rav Pappa replied, "Thus said Rava: The halakha follows Beit Hillel." He did not lie, for Rava indeed ruled that the halakha follows Beit Hillel, but not in the specific manner that applied to Rav Pappa’s case. And thus, he acted in such a way to avoid directly addressing the issue.
