Verse 2

(1) TAKE A CENSUS.  To order them so that they can enter the Land immediately, each one by his ensign, without war -- rather, that the nations would vacate before them, as some did, as he [Isaiah] testified, when he said (Isa. 17:9), "like the deserted sites that the Horesh and the Amir abandoned because of the Israelites."  They may have been the Girgashite clans who up and left on their own.  And with the going astray of the spies, the seven nations committed further evil, for 40 years, and it became necessary to destroy them.  
(2) IN ENUMERATING THE NAMES.  Because at that time, each member of that generation was considered based on his/her name, which indicated his/her personal character to their glory, in the manner of (Exod. 33:17), "I have known you by name."  But it did not happen that way for the generation that entered the Land, and so they were not counted in a numbering by name, and so only the heads of the clans and the census totals were mentioned.  And along with this, it informs us that the original intent was that these men themselves would live and take ownership of the Land, and none of them would be lost.

EACH ONE THE HEAD OF HIS ANCESTRAL HOUSE.  The reason that each one shall be with you is that each one is the head of his ancestral house, and no one will be able to refute his statements about the family connection of each person.

Verse 6

BREAKING FAITH WITH ADONAI.  The tradition has already made clear that this refers to theft from a convert, for indeed, one who steals from him profanes the Name of his God in the eyes of the convert, who has come to take refuge under His wings, and therefore, he is called "one who breaks faith with the sacred," and he is liable for an asham, in accordance with the law of anyone who breaks faith with Him.

Verse 7



Verse 8

WHICH IS RETURNED TO ADONAI.  For when the owners are no longer in existence, it is appropriate to return the stolen amount to the master of the owners, that being God, may He be blessed, as our Sages said, [if one has taken a pledge from a slave, and] if the slave dies, he should return it to his master.

Verse 9

ANY GIFT [KOL T'RUMAH].  The tradition has already established that the "t'rumah" mentioned here is the bikurim [first fruits], which are presented to God, may He be blessed, as is customary [for a tenant farmer] to present first fruits to the landowner, as it says (Deut. 26:3), "I acknowledge this day before Adonai your God that I have entered the land that Adonai swore to our fathers to assign us."  And it says here that, just as in the case of the theft from a convert that is returned to God, He gives it to the priest of the [weekly] roster, so in the case of the bikkurim that are presented to God, may He be blessed, He gives them to the priest of the roster.

Verse 10

(1) AND EACH PERSON SHALL RETAIN HIS SACRED DONATIONS.  But other holy gifts, i.e., t'rumot and tithes, even though they are consecrated to Adonai, they do not belong to any particular priest, but rather to whichever priest the owner gives them.  
(2) EACH PERSON WHO GIVES TO A PRIEST.  But when any owner gives any sacred donation to a priest, it will no longer be in the category of "his" sacred donation [i.e., the owner's], but "it will be his" -- the priest's who receives it, and neither the owner nor any other priest can take it away from him.

(1) WHEN YOU MOUNT [ELEVATE] THE LAMPS -- When you light the six lamps, -- 
(2)TOWARDS THE FACE OF [JPS: AT THE FRONT OF] THE LAMPSTAND, which is the central arm, and this is when you direct the flame of each of the six lamps toward the central arm, then --
(3) THE SEVEN LAMPS WILL GIVE LIGHT [JPS: LET THE SEVEN LAMPS GIVE LIGHT] -- all seven will illumine, and will bring down the supernal light for, Israel.  For they teach that, since the light of the right-side lamps and the light of the left-side lamps is directed towards and faces the light of the central arm, which is the essence of the lampstand, and thus it is appropriate that the direction of the right-side lamps, which pertain to eternal life, and the left-side lamps, which deal with transient life, which assist the right-side lamps, as they said (Hull. 92a), "Let the clusters pray for the leaves, for were it not for the leaves the clusters could not exist," shall satisfy the will of God, may He be blessed, in a manner that, through both of them, His intent, and they will exalt His name together, as they had accepted on themselves when they testified, saying (Exod. 19:8), "All those assembled answered as one, saying, 'All that Adonai has spoken we will do,'" meaning, "among all of us put together, we will fulfill His will."

Verse 3



Verse 4

NOW THIS IS HOW THE LAMPSTAND WAS MADE: IT WAS HAMMERED WORK. This, the very purpose that was the intent of the lighting of the lamps "towards the face of the lampstand," is itself the intent of the obligation of having the lampstand be of beaten work, to teach the unity that was an intended purpose of [the lampstand] itself.

Verse 2

(1) SEND MEN.  Do not let them [the people] send, as they proposed to do, in saying (Deut. 1:22), "Let's send men ahead," lest they send ordinary men, who are not familiar with the good qualities of the land, and who would then speak unfavorably about it, such that Israel would think that God was mistaken, and they would not repent as they in fact did later, saying (Deut. 1:41), "We have sinned against Adonai."  And this because the spies whom Moshe sent, even though they sinned in diverting the minds of the people, out of a lack of faith in God Almighty, they did recognize and recount the good quality of the land, in their saying (v.27), "and it does indeed ooze milk and honey."  And so he testifies in his saying (Deut. 1:25), "They took some of the fruit of the land with them ... And they said, 'It is a good land,'" but they said it was impossible to conquer it.  When Israel recognized their sin, in that they did not trust in God, may He be exalted, and His triumph after He had done wondrous things with them, they repented and said (Deut. 1:41), "We have sinned against Adonai; we will go up now and fight."  And they prayed, as it says (Deut. 1:45), "You repented and wept before Adonai," but God, may He be blessed, did not accept their prayer because they committed a Desecration of God, for which death is the only atonement, as He testifies about them when He said (Exod. 32:34), "But when I make an accounting, I will bring them to account, etc." 
(2) EACH ONE A CHIEFTAIN AMONG THEM.  Each one the best among his tribe in recognizing the nature of the land.

It mentions them in order by their ages, since they were at that time all equal in their merits, in particular with respect to [expertise connected to] their mission, not in the order [of the birth] of the tribes, and not in the order of the ensigns [the arrangement of the tribes camped around the Mishkan].

THIS IS THE TORAH LAW THAT ADONAI HAS COMMANDED.  In saying (Num. 8:7), "Sprinkle on them water of purification."  And the Sages have already said, "This is why 'hukah' is written regarding it -- it is a decree from Me; you may not question it."  And Sh'lomo said about it (Eccl. 7:23), "I thought I could fathom it, but it eludes me."  And among the principal elements of this obscurity is that it contaminates the pure and purifies the contaminated.  It is true that, when we look into the mitzva as a whole, then perhaps a word will come in stealth, and we will catch a whisper of it (see Job 4:12).  And this is what we find:      FIRST, all those who deal with it, from the time of its burning and forward, are impure, these being the one who burns; the one who casts cedar wood, hyssop, and crimson thread into the fire; the one who gathers [the ashes]; the one who touches [the water of purification or an impure person]; and the one who carries [the water].  However, the one who sprinkles [the water] and the one who sanctifies are pure.    SECOND, among the main principles of the heifer is that it be entirely red.  And the prophet has already made clear that sin is analogized to red, in his saying (Isa. 1:18), "Though your sins be like crimson, they can turn snow-white."  That is why the Sages said that they would tie a strip of red at the entrance of the Temple; if it turned white, they would rejoice; if not, they would be sad.     THIRD, one should ponder that, while (Prov. 30:6), "The word of God is entirely pure," it doubtless aligns actions toward the Middle Path, for each of the two extremes is repugnant, as he said (Prov. 28:18), "But he who is crooked in his two ways will fall in one of them."    FOURTH, one should ponder that there is no good way to straighten the one "who is crooked in his two ways" and to restore him to the middle path except to turn him toward the opposite extreme, as occurs with bodily ills, as it says (Prov. 20:30), "Bruises and wounds are repayment for evil," even though that turning toward the extreme is, in and of itself, repugnant, and would be destructive of one who is on the middle path, like the laxative that benefits the sick but damages the healthy.      FIFTH, one should ponder that there is no role for the heifer's ashes in purifying any kind of impurity other than the impurity of the dead body.  And it is well known that the Torah and mitzvot "are life to him who finds them" (Prov. 4:22) and to those who engage with them, as it says (Deut. 32:47), "it is your very life."  And one who turns away from them towards frivolities is going towards death, or even actually dies, as they said (Ber. 18b), "the evil ones even during their lives are called dead."    SIXTH, one should ponder what [the Sages] said, that the cedar symbolizes pride, and the hyssop its opposite, and, by the crimson thread being with them, it symbolizes that both of them are sins, as they said (Sotah 5a), "A Torah scholar who has arrogance should be excommunicated, and one who does not have arrogance at all should be excommunicated as well," particularly for one who must exercise authority for the public good.  And they have already said that Shaul was punished because he did not insist on the respect due him, as it says (I Sam. 10:27), "they scorned him and brought him no gift. But he pretended not to mind."  And the prophet [Sh'muel] grasped this in saying to him (I Sam. 15:17), "You may look small to yourself, but you are the head of the tribes of Israel."    IF SO, we shall say that, while the mitzva is a "hukah," and we may neither question it nor doubt whether it is proper or not, for every saying of God is refined. and has an elevated rationale that is certainly known to the King Who commanded it, and perhaps also to Moshe our teacher and those like him, there is still in it some hint as to the necessary way to respond to every sinner, which is that he should lean to the extreme that is the opposite of his actions that contaminate every pure mind, so that he can attain the middle way and become pure.  And this path, though it is good and purifying for the sinner, it is degraded, evil, and contaminating for every pure mind, as [the Sages] said (Ta'an. 11a) [regarding the nazir, who must bring a sin offering at the end of his term], "Against which soul did he sin? He denied himself wine."  It is true that the water of purification, composed of burnt dust and water, which are two extremes from which a middle way can be created, indicates that from this middle way there can be a correction of the sinner, which is called "purification," as it says (Lev. 16:30), "From all of your sins, you will be pure before Adonai."      AND from what is appropriate to ponder what He decreed, that (v.13) one who touches a dead body contaminates God's Tabernacle, and (vv. 14-20) who enters a tent [under which a dead body lies] contaminates God's sanctuary. And this is because one who approaches the frivolities of the world doubtless contaminates the pure mind which is the Tabernacle of God's sanctuary, which is called "the image of God," and his impurity is from an error of omission.  And one who enters the tent of the dead body is like the dwellers in the houses of materiality, and there is nothing before them but their bodies, from transitory and fragile life, descending to death, and this doubtless contaminates God's sanctuary, where the people and the individual are holy to his God, as it says (Deut. 26:19), "you shall be a people holy to Adonai your God," and then he contaminates with an error of commission, the opposite of [God's original] intent.  And thus it is appropriate that impurity via a tent occur through none other than a Jewish dead person, since his material [i.e., body] alone is eligible and ready from among all others, for the service of God, may He be blessed; he alone sins against the honored [soul?].  However, the sages, who instruct people about the way [to correct errors], are like the one who sprinkles and the one who sanctifies, and impurity does not attach to them at all.  And thus there is, in the details of the observance of this mitzva, as it is written and as it comes through the tradition, a hint of these [matters], which are doubtless intended by the Torah.

BALAK SAW.  [He was] a man famous for his knowledge of military matters, as it says (Jud. 11:25), "are you any better than Balak son of Zippor, etc.?"  He saw that, when Israel sought to cross Sihon's land, and he did not allow them, and he saw that they conquered not through military strategems.

Verse 3

(1) MOAB WAS ALARMED.  The "rams" [leaders] of Moab, who did not allow Israel to pass, as Yiftah testified, in saying (Jud. 11:17), "They also sent a mission to the king of Moab, and he refused." 
(2) BECAUSE THAT PEOPLE WAS SO NUMEROUS.  Not because of any military strategem.  ...
(3) MOAB DREADED.  The multitudes of Moab despaired of their lives BECAUSE OF THE ISRAELITES who were plundering them.

Verse 4

(1) NOW that they have conquered the territories of Sihon and Og, --
(2) THIS HORDE WILL LICK CLEAN ALL THAT IS ABOUT US to expand and secure their borders.  
(3) AND BALAK SON OF ZIPPOR was known as a mighty man and a warrior --
(4) WAS KING OF MOAB AT THAT TIME, yet it did not enter his mind to make war against Israel, as it says (Jud. 11:25), "Did he start a quarrel with Israel or go to war with them?"  And as for what Yehoshua said (Josh. 24:9), "Thereupon Balak son of Zippor, the king of Moab, made ready to attack Israel," he was referring to his [Balak's] hiring of Bil'am to curse, as he made clear in saying (ibid.), "He sent for Balaam son of Beor."

Verse 5



Verse 6

(1) PERHAPS I CAN, after you curse him --
(2) WE WILL STRIKE HIM, I with war and you with cursing.  ...
(3) WHOMEVER YOU BLESS IS BLESSED.  Actually, [Bil'am's] power was not to bless, but rather to curse, by mentioning [the enemy's] sin, or by aligning the time [of his cursing with a propitious time], as our Rabbis said.  And therefore, [Balak] did not ask him to bless him to conquer or to be able to withstand [Israel], but he said "I know that whomever you bless is blessed" out of respect for Bil'am and to show that he did not think of him purely as a destructive force.

Verse 12

MY COVENANT OF PEACE.  From the Angel of Death.  As in (Job 25:2) "He imposes peace in His heights."  For it is true that a deficiency occurs only through a clash of opposites.  This [peace] was fulfilled with Pinhas, who lived a much longer life than all the other persons of his generation, until he served in the Mishkan at Shilo at the time of the Concubine of Giv'ah, which was undoubtably after the death of Yehoshua and the other elders who lived after Yehoshua.  And even more so if [the Concubine of Giv'ah] was at the time of Yiftah, who wrote to the king of the Ammonites, "While Israel has been inhabiting Heshbon and its dependencies ... for three hundred years."  And [our Sages] have already related that Pinhas did not want to go to Yiftah to release his vow.  And even more so according to the one who says that Eliahu is Pinhas, he being still alive.

Verse 13

(1) bECAUSE HE TOOK IMPASSIONED ACTION FOR HIS GOD.  And because he has fought my fight, I will save him from a fight with any opponent, and he will have peace.  
(2) THUS MAKING EXPIATION FOR THE ISRAELITES.  By exacting vengeance in the sight of all of them, so that atonement would be effected for them for their not having protested against the sinners.  Therefore he is worthy of everlasting priesthood, through which he can make atonement for them.

Verse 14

THE NAME OF THE ISRAELITE MAN WHO WAS KILLED.   This [the identifying of the individuals, or the identifying of them at this point rather than when they committed their sin] is because, indeed, in his effecting atonement in this manner, he exposed himself to danger in that, in front of everyone, he killed a prince and a king's daughter.
