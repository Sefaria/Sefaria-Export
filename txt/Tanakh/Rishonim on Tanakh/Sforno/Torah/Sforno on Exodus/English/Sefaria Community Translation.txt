Verse 17

(1) NOW ON THE LETTING GO ... GOD DID NOT LEAD THEM BY WAY OF THE LAND OF THE PHILISTINES.  Even though the original divine intent had been to guide Israel to Mt. Sinai to receive the Torah and from there to the Land of Israel, as He said (Exod. 6:7-9), "And I will take you to be My people, ... and I will bring you into the land," still, the intent now was to guide them to the Sea of Reeds, which was not the way to either of these [Mt. Sinai or the Land of Israel], and this in order to drown Par'oh and his army, in the manner of (Jud. 4:7), "'And I will draw Sisera, toward you up to the Wadi Kishon; and I will deliver him into your hands.'"   Now the direct and short route from Egypt to the Sea of Reeds was by way of the Philistine territory, and it says that God, may He be blessed, did not want to lead them by that path --
(2) BECAUSE [JPS: ALTHOUGH] IT WAS NEARER.  Because that route and its end were close to Egypt, there would be many travelers there, and they would tell [the news] from there to Egypt and from Egypt to there --
(3) AND WHEN THEY SEE WAR. When they hear that Par'oh is preparing himself to pursue them, THE PEOPLE WILL HAVE A CHANGE OF HEART certainly out of fear of their war, and they will return to Egypt, and therefore He diverted them to a route where people did not go.

Verse 22

כי אם שמוע תשמע, My relationship to you then will not be protective, defensive, i.e. that I need to protect you, but aggressive, by treating your enemies as My enemies. This is the reverse of G’d’s attitude to Nineveh in Jonah 4,11 where G’d asked Jonah: “how can I not relate protectively to a city of over 120.000 people?”

דבר אל בני ישראל ויקחו לי תרומה, tell the stewards of Israel that I desire that they should collect a contribution for Me. [the Torah had not specified “who” should take. Ed.] Moses also issued such instructions on his return from the mountain the third time as the Torah reports (Exodus 34,32) ואחרי כן נגשו כל בני ישראל, ויצום את כל אשר דבר ה' אתו בהר סיני, “after that all the people of Israel drew near, and he commanded them all that the Lord had talked about with him on Mount Sinai.” This was followed by ויאמר משה אל כל עדת בני ישראל קחו מאתכם תרומה in 35,4. The עדת בני ישראל who were to carry out this “taking (accepting) of the gifts were the Sanhedrin, the High Court. The Israelites were so anxious to contribute that they did not wait until the High Court organised all this, but they approached Moses personally bringing with them so many contributions that Moses was forced to call a halt to this (36,5). This was also the reason that there was nothing left for the princes of Israel (the tribal chiefs) to contribute other than the gemstones for Aaron’s breastplate and the various oils making up the “oil of anointing.”
מאת כל איש, G’d commanded that the procedure should not be like the imposition of every man’s contribution for the public charity fund which was treated as a tax. Contributions were to be accepted only from volunteers.

AND A HIN OF OLIVE OIL.  Undoubtedly, this small amount of oil did not suffice to anoint the whole amount of spices, but they would cook the oil with the water in which the spices were cooked, or they would put the oil on the surface of that cooking water, until the water was exhausted by the cooking, either by cooking or by the lesser heat from within the water, and the oil would remain, like "the product of the expert spice blenders" (v.25).  In this regard, there is a disagreement between our Sages whether there was enough [oil] to cook it with the cooking water, or whether to put the oil on the surface of the cooking water and to melt it with the steam through the lesser heat.

Verse 21

(1) THESE ARE THE ITEMS [JPS: RECORDS] OF THE TABERNACLE.  Each of the components of the Tabernacle that are recorded above [in Parashat VaYak'hel] are the "p'kudim" about which it is said (Num. 4:32-33), "you shall list by name the objects that are their porterage tasks ... under the direction of Itamar."  And this is because each of them was worthy to be considered and to be called by name in its individual capacity and not only as part of this category.  This is all the more correct with respect to the sacred utensils in the porterage responsibility of the Kehatites.  For this reason, they did not deteriorate, as they [the Sages] said (Sukk. 45b), "Lest you say that [after the secreting of the Tabernacle] their hope is lost and their prospect is abolished, the verse states: 'Acacia wood, standing' -- they stand forever and for all time."  Also, none of them fell into the enemy's hands, by contrast to what happened with the Temple of Shlomo and its vessels, as is made clear in the destruction of the First Temple by Nevuzaradan, for there is no mention there [e.g., II Kings 25:13-17] of items from the Tabernacle of Moshe our Teacher.  
(2) THE TABERNACLE OF THE PACT.  It [Scripture] recounts the superior qualities of the Tabernacle, for which it could remain permanent and not fall into the enemy's hands.  First, that it was the Tabernacle of the Pact, that the Tablets of the Pact were in it.  Second, that it was "drawn up at Moshe's bidding."  Third, that it was the responsibility of the Levites under the direction of Itamar, for in fact the responsibility for all parts of the Tabernacle was under Itamar.   Fourth (v.22), "Now Bezalel, son of Uri son of Hur, of the tribe of Judah, had made," for the chief artisans of the Tabernacle and its vessels were among the elite and the righteous of the generation, and so the Sh'china rested on what they made, and it did not fall into the enemy's hands.  But Shlomo's Temple, for which those who did the work were from Tyre, even though the Sh'china rested on it, its components deteriorated, and it was necessary to do restorative work, and it ultimately fell into the enemy's hands.  But the Second Temple, which lacked all of these attributes, the Sh'china did not rest on it, and it fell into the enemy's hands.  For indeed, the Second Temple was not the Tabernacle of the Pact, for the Tablets of the Pact were not there, and it was "drawn up" only at Koresh's bidding, and the Levites were not there [at the construction], as Ezra testified when he said (Ezra 8:15), "I reviewed the people and the priests, but I did not find any Levites there," and among those who engaged in its construction, there were Sidonites and Tyrites, as is explained in the Book of Ezra.
