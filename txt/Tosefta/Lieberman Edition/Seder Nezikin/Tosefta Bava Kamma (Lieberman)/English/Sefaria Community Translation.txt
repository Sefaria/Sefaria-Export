A camel laden with flax passing through the public domain, and its flax entered a store and caught fire from the storekeeper's lamp, and lit the building... (M. Bava Kama 6:6) — [from the doorstop inwads, the camel's owner is liable], from the doorstop outwards, the storekeeper is liable. R. Yehuda says, if a portico was before it which kindled, he is exempt. But the sages say, even if a portico was before it which kindled, he is liable. And thus R. Yehuda says that with a Ḥanukkah lamp he is exempt, since it was placed with permission. But the sages say in either case he is liable. See also, those who build sukkot at the opening of their stores in the public space for the festival, even though they are permitted to do so, if someone comes and is injured by it they are liable.
