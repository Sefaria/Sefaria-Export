Tosefta Oholot



Chapter 1

It is the same whether one touches the tent [covering a corpse], or erects [the tent], or moves [the tent]: All of them are counted [as having been defiled through contact] with a corpse. Only a person can defile by carrying a corpse. Food and drink and vessels do not defile by being carried by (i.e., supported by the body of) a corpse. Said Rabbi Yosei, in the same way, you could say, "Anything that is defiled for seven days by a corpse is [only] defiled until the evening by a zav, [and] anything that is defiled until the evening by corpse is completely pure by a zav." How so? [If] one person [touches] another person, there are two [degrees of impurity] by a corpse<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote"><b>במת</b> (MS Vienna, GR"A)</i>, and one [degree of impurity] by a zav. A vessel [that touches] another vessel: Three by a corpse and two by a zav. A vessel [that touches] a person [who touches] another vessel: Four by a corpse and three by a zav. When do these words apply? In regards to terumah and consecrated foods, but a nazirite only shaves [when he is defiled] by a corpse. And they are only liable for [causing] impurity to the Temple and its consecrated items on account of a corpse. Said Rabbi Akiva, I have a fifth [degree of impurity]: The tent-peg affixed to the tent (Mishnah Oholot 1:3). They said to him, the tent and the tent-peg are one and the same.
An idolater, and an animal, and an eight year-old, and an earthenware vessel, and food<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote"><b>וכלי חרס ואוכלים</b> (see GR"A)</i>, and liquids, [any of] which touch a corpse: Vessels that touch them are pure. And just as a limb of a corpse, or a limb [severed] from a living person that lacks the minimum quantity of bone is pure in regards to a human, so too with carrion and with <i>sheratzim</i>, [such a limb] is pure. A limb that does not have the appropriate amount of flesh on it (Mishnah Oholot 1:8): In regards to a human, it is impure; in regards to carrion and <i>sheratzim</i>, it is impure.<br>The brain, whether of a human, a domesticated animal, a wild animal, fowl, carrion, <i>tereifot</i>, detestable things (i.e., insects), or creeping things: It is impure, and they are liable on account of it for <i>piggul</i> [and] <i>notar</i>, and it is impure and defiles with an olive's-bulk in regards to carrying, and with a lentil's-bulk in regards to touching. The general rule of the matter: The brain, behold, is like flesh in every respect.
[There are] nine [limbs] in the head, and the jaw is among them. Five in the knee, two on above and two below, and the protuberance in the middle. Anything that has tendons and bones, behold, that is a limb, and anything that does not have tendons and bones is not a limb.  And one with extra [limbs] causes the count to rise. One missing [limbs], who only has two hundred, and one with extra [limbs], who has two hundred eighty: All of them join together for [the purpose of reaching the threshold of] one hundred twenty-five (see Mishnah Oholot 2:1).<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote">In other words, regardless of whether a given person has a greater or lesser number of limbs than the standard count of 248, the threshold of 125 (constituting the "majority of the bones") remains constant for purposes of corpse-impurity. (See Bekh. 45a:16 (Steinsaltz).)</i> One who is [still] alive: Neither [the principle of] "the majority of the [skeletal] structure" nor that of "the majority of the bones" apply.

Chapter 2

A Jew who slaughtered an impure animal for a Gentile, [and] he slaughtered it [by cutting] two (i.e., the two <i>simanim</i>, the trachea and esophagus), or the majority of two, and [the animal] is still twitching, it defiles with the impurity of food, but not with the impurity of carrion. A limb that is separated from it is like one that separates from the living (i.e., a live animal), and flesh that separates from it is like [flesh] that separates from the living, and it is forbidden to Noachides, and even if the animal dies. [If it was killed] by stabbing, it [only] defiles with the impurity of food. [If] he slaughtered it with something that rendered it a <i>tereifa</i>, it has no impurity whatsoever.<br>And a Gentile who slaughtered a pure animal for a Jew, and slaughtered it [by cutting] two or the majority of two, and it is still twitching, it defiles with the impurity of food but not with the impurity of carrion. And a limb that separates from it is like one that separates from the living, and flesh that separates from it is like [flesh] that separates from the living, and it is forbidden to Noachides, and even if the animal dies. [If it was killed] by stabbing, it defiles with the impurity of food. [If] he slaughtered [only] one [<i>siman</i>], or the majority of one, it has no impurity whatsoever. [If] he slaughtered it with something that does not render it a <i>tereifa</i>, and a Jew came and completed [the slaughter], it is permitted to eat. [If] a Jew slaughtered two or the majority of two with [something that does not render the animal a <i>tereifa</i>], it is the same for both the Gentile and the Jew: He may cut flesh from it, and wait until it dies, and [the cut flesh] is permitted for eating.
The "ladleful of corpse-mold" that they spoke of (Mishnah Oholot 2:1) exists from the finger-joints and upwards, the words of Rabbi Meir. And the Sages say, from the hollow of his hand. And what is a corpse that has corpse-mold? One who is buried naked in a stone coffin on top of the floor, or on top of a marble slab, but one who is buried with his clothing and in a wooden coffin on top of dirt does not have corpse-mold. And they remove dirt from under him, and that is "dirt of the graves," a ladleful and [a little] more (ibid.). Mixed blood<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote">i.e., some of which flowed out before death (which is pure), and some of which flowed out after death (which is impure)</i> that is found in a grave but it is unknown what kind [of blood] it is: That is "dirt of the graves," a ladleful and [a little] more. Rabbi Elazar b'Rabbi Tzadok explained, you separate the pebbles and the wood chips, and you remove that which is certainly [not dirt of the grave], and you leave that which is doubtful, and that is "dirt of the graves," a ladleful and [a little] more.
A spinal column, the majority of whose vertebrae were sawed off: Even though the structure [of the spine] remains intact, it is pure. [But] whenever it is inside a grave, even if it is broken, and even if it is crushed, it is impure, because the grave joins it together [with other sources of impurity].
A skull that has a long puncture-wound, or that had many puncture-wounds: They combine together [to constitute] the size of a drill hole. Said Rabbi Yosei ben HaMeshullam, it so happened that in Ein Bul, there was one whose skull had [a piece] broken off, and the doctor brought him a patch made of a pumpkin shell, and he survived. Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar said to him, that is no proof. Even though he survived all the days of that year, once there was a rainfall, he caught cold on it (i.e., the patched skull), and he died.
An olive's bulk of flesh that separates from a limb of the living (i.e., a living person): Rabbi Eliezer says, it transmits impurity. They came back to Rabbi Eliezer with three (alt., "two" (GR"A)) responses. "No. If you are comparing this situation to a corpse<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote"><b>במת</b>; alt., <b> בבית</b> ("of a house") (Tosefta Eduyot 2:10)</i> that [transmits impurity if it] possesses the greater part of a quarter-kav of corpse-mold<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote"><b>רוב רובע רקב</b>; alt., <b>רוב ורובע ורקב</b> ("the majority [of the skeletal structure or number of limbs] or a quarter-kav or corpse-mold) (see GR"A; the same emendation is offered wherever this phrase occurs)</i>, can you say the same regarding a limb of the living, which does not [transmit impurity if it] possesses [the greater part of] a quarter-kav of corpse-mold?" Another matter: "Who dangles from whom? Does the limb of the living dangle from the flesh, or does the flesh dangle from the limb? The flesh dangles from the limb! Can it be possible that the flesh would transmit impurity by touch and by carrying and by [being under] a tent, while the limb [itself] remains pure?" Said Rabbi Shimon: "I would be astonished if Rabbi Eliezer [really did] render [an olive's bulk of flesh from the limb of the living] impure. Rather, he did not render it impure except in a situation where the limb possesses a sufficient quantity of flesh, such that both this and that (i.e., the limb and the flesh) may transmit impurity by touch and carrying and by [being under] a tent.
A bone the size of a barleycorn that separates from a limb of the living: Rabbi Nechunia renders it impure. They came back to Rabbi Nechunia with three (alt., "two" (GR"A)) responses. "No. If you are comparing this situation to a corpse that [transmits impurity if it] possesses [the greater part of] a quarter-kav of corpse-mold, can you say the same regarding a limb of the living, which does not [transmit impurity if it] possesses [the greater part of] a quarter-kav of corpse-mold?" Another matter: "Who dangles from whom? Does the limb of the living dangle from the flesh, or does the flesh dangle from the limb? The flesh dangles from the limb! Can it be possible that the flesh would transmit impurity by touch and by carrying and by [being under] a tent, while the limb [itself] remains pure?" Said Rabbi Shimon: "I would be astonished if Rabbi Nechunia [really did] render [a bone the size of a barleycorn that separates from the limb of the living] impure. Rather, he did not render it impure except in a situation where the limb [itself] possesses a bone the size of a barleycorn, such that both this and that (i.e., the limb and the flesh) may transmit impurity by touch and carrying and by [being under] a tent. Rabbi Yehoshua responded to the words of both of them: "Just as just as the living has 248 limbs, bone and flesh that separate from it are pure, does it not follow that [in regards to] a limb, which does not contain 248 [limbs within it], that the bone and flesh and separate from it should be pure?" Rebbi responded to the words of Rabbi Yehoshua: "No. If you hold that in regards to [bones and flesh that] separate from the living, that they certainly separated from something that is pure, can you say the same as to that which separates from a limb [of the living], which certainly separated from something that is impure?"

Chapter 3

Said Rabbi Yehuda: Rabbi Akiva and the Sages did not disagree about a quarter-<i>log</i> of blood that separated from two corpses, that it is pure. About what did they disagree? About a quarter-<i>log</i> of blood that separated from two quarter-<i>logs</i> from two corpses, for Rabbi Akiva renders it impure and the Sages render it pure. Rabbi Shimon ben Yehuda says in the name of Rabbi Shimon, Rabbi Akiva and the Sages did not disagree about a quarter-<i>log</i> of blood that separated from two quarter-<i>logs</i> from two corpses, that is is impure. About what did they disagree? About a quarter-<i>log</i> of blood that separated from two corpses, for Rabbi Akiva renders it impure and the Sages render it pure (see Mishnah Oholot 2:2).
Abba Shaul says, a quarter-<i>log</i> is the initial [quantity of] blood of a child. The blood of a child, all of which flowed out and there is not a quarter-<i>log</i>: Rabbi Akiva says, [it is impure in] any amount, and the Sages say, [there must be] a quarter-<i>log</i> (Mishnah Oholot 2:2). For Rabbi Akiva says, impurity is in the blood and impurity is in the bones. Just as bones are impure even if they are less than a quarter-kav, so too blood is impure even if it is less than a quarter-<i>log</i>. They came back to Rabbi Akiva with three responses. "No. If you hold that as to bones, where the majority [of the bones of the body] are impure even if they are less than a quarter-kav, could you say the same regarding blood, which is only impure if there is a quarter-<i>log</i>?" Another matter: "No. If  you hold that as to bones, where even a minority of bones can be impure [if they comprise the majority of the skeletal structure], and [even] a bone the size of a barleycorn that separates from them is impure, could you say the same regarding blood, which is only impure if there is a quarter-<i>log</i>?" Another matter: "No. If you hold that as to bones, where it is known that all of them are in front of you, could you say the same regarding blood? For we say that if even the smallest drop is left over, it is pure." He said to them, "Even I only hold such in a situation where all of it came out." They said to him, "That is impossible."
A quarter-kav of bones from the majority of the skeleton in size, and bones of (i.e., comprising) the majority of the skeleton in size, even though they do not contain a quarter-kav, are impure. A quarter-kav of bones from the majority of the skeleton in quantity, and bones of the majority of the skeleton in quantity, even though they do not contain a quarter-kav, are impure. Rabbi Yehuda stated a different version [of the foregoing halacha]: Beit Shammai say, a quarter-kav of bones from the skeleton from the structural majority or from numerical majority, [or] the structural majority or numerical majority of a corpse (i.e., considering the limbs as opposed to the bones), even though they do not contain a quarter-kav, are impure. And what is its "structural [majority]"? The calves and the thighs, and the ribs, and the spinal column. And what is its "numerical [majority]"? The digits of its hands and its feet, as long as they comprise [one hundred] twenty-five.<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote">There are many variants to the above; this translation generally follows Rav Shimon of Sens. Note that Mishnah Oholot 1:8 states that there are six bones per finger/toe (thirty per hand/foot), yielding 120; Beit Shammai may have had a different opinion or the received text may be corrupt.</i> Said Rabbi Yehoshua, I can reconcile the words of Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel [as follows]: One from the calves and from the thighs. It comes out to be the structural majority in size; and half of the numerical majority, and half of the structural majority do not join together [for purposes of impurity]. The equivalent of half of an olive's-bulk of flesh and half of an olive's-bulk of decomposed corpse matter do join with one another, and the remainder of all the impurities from a corpse do not join with one another, because their measurements are not equivalent.
The tombstone (i.e. a large stone laid horizontally over a grave, akin to a roof) and the stone frame supporting the tombstone defile by touch and by a tent, but they do not defile by carrying. Rabbi Eliezer says, they defile by carrying, from a logical deduction (<i>kal v'chomer</i>), for if they defile by touch, which is light, should they not defile [by carrying], which is stringent? Rabbi Akiva said to him, "If they defile by touch, which is more common, should they defile by carrying, which is less common?" Rabbi Eliezer said to him, "What is this, Akiva? For isn't it true that tent-impurity is less common than both of them, yet behold, they defile in it. And if they defile in a tent, which is less common, should they not defile by carrying, which is more common?" Rabbi Akiva was dismissed. Then Rabbi Yehoshua jumped up. He said to him, "Tent-impurity is more common than carrying-impurity, for one who tents over a corpse with a sukkah is impure, while one who moves it is pure." Said Rabbi Shimon, this is how it is judged: If there is a sukkah with an entryway, behold, it interposes on top of [the corpse], and if there is a sukkah without an entryway, that is itself a "tombstone." Rather, this is how it one should consider it: Tent-impurity is more common than carrying-impurity, for everything is defiled in a tent, and only a person is defiled by carrying. § It so happened in Beit Dagan in Judea that there was someone who died on the evening before Passover, and they went to bury  him and the women<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote"><b>הנשים</b> (Zar Zahav)</i> entered and they tied the rope to the tombstone. Then the men pulled from outside [and lowered the body into the grave] and the women entered to bury him, and then the men left and made their Pesach offerings in the evening [in a state of purity].
Two large stones of four by four handbreadths each that were made into a tombstone: One who tents over both of them is impure. [If] he removed one of them, one who tents over the second one is pure, because that impurity has a means of escape (see Mishnah Oholot 3:6).

Chapter 4

Rabbi Yosei says, a ladleful of corpse-mold defiles by touch and by carrying and by tenting. Rabbi Shimon says, three impurities spread out from a corpse, each one of which defiles in two [ways] and does not [defile] in the third [way]. And they are: The tombstone, and the frame supporting the tombstone, and a bone the size of a barleycorn, [and] a ladleful of corpse-mold. The tombstone and the frame supporting the tombstone defile by touch and by airspace (=tenting), but they do not defile by carrying. And where [is impurity imparted] by carrying? By a bone the size of a barleycorn. A bone the size of a barleycorn defiles by touch and by carrying<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote"><b>במשא</b> not <b>באויר</b> ("by airspace")</i>, but it does not defile by tenting. And where [is impurity imparted] by tenting? By a ladleful of corpse-mold. A ladleful of corpse-mold defiles by carrying and by tenting, but it does not defile by touch. And so too would Rabbi Yosei say, an olive's-bulk from a corpse that was divided even into ten [pieces] defiles by carrying and by tenting, but does not defile by touch.
Said Rabbi Yehuda: There were six things that Rabbi Akiva had rendered impure but then changed his mind about. It so happened that they brought boxes of bones from K'far Tavi, and they they set them down in the open-air [courtyard] of the synagogue of Lod. And Theodoros the Physician entered, and all the other physicians with him, and they said, "There is neither a spine of a corpse or a skull of a corpse here." They said, "Since there are those that render [such incomplete corpses] impure and there are those that render them pure, we will stand up and take a vote." They started from Rabbi Akiva and he declared them pure. They said to him, "Since you had rendered pure what you had [previously] rendered impure, they will be pure." Said Rabbi Shimon, "[Rather,] until the day of Rabbi Akiva's death, he rendered it impure, and if he changed his mind after his death, I haven't heard of it."
The fat of a corpse that was complete and they cut it up is impure. [If] it was scattered around and they melted it<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote"><b>והתיכו</b> (GR"A)</i>, it is pure, for that which is joined by human [agency] is not [considered] joined [for purposes of impurity]. An etrog that burst open, and they stuck it [back together] with a reed or a splinter of wood is not [considered] joined, for that which is joined by human [agency] is not [considered] joined. A marrow-bone of a corpse that was fashioned into the handle of a knife, behold, that is not [considered] joining by human [agency]. [If] one joined an olive's bulk of a corpse that had split into two, it is impure, and one need not be concerned lest it is deficient. An etrog that was cut into two is pure, and one need not be concerned lest it is deficient. A grain-offering that was mixed by five priests is fit [for the Altar], and one need not be concerned lest it is deficient. [If] it was transferred from vessel to vessel it [remains] fit. Rabban Shimon ben Gamaliel says, if he apportioned it [among the priests], behold, it is disqualified.<br>The equivalent of an olive's bulk from a corpse, and the equivalent of an olive's bulk of decayed matter, a ladleful of corpse-mold from two corpses join together with one another. A ladleful of corpse-mold: Whenever there is even a small amount of dirt in it, it is impure, and Rabbi Yehoshua renders it pure because it was neutralized.
A quarter-<i>log</i> of blood that was absorbed into clothing -- how is it measured? One brings a large amount of water and launders [the clothing] in it, and brings another [container of] water and pours a quarter-<i>log</i> of blood into it. If they look the same (i.e., the shade of red is the same), behold, it is impure, and if not, it is pure. Rabbi Yosei says, the earlier Elders say, from the downward slope and within, the house is impure, and from the downward slope and outward, the house is pure (see Mishnah Oholot 3:3). They came back and said, as to a slope, whether it is from the inside or from the outside, the house is pure. [If] the corpse is outside and his hair is inside, even though there is only the smallest amount under the tent, the house and one who overtents it is impure. 
All fluids from a corpse are pure except for its blood. And all who see the blood of a corpse, behold, they are impure.
And what is "mixed blood" (see Mishnah Oholot 3:5)? Rabbi Elazar ben Yehuda says, one who was killed, from whom a quarter-<i>log</i> of blood came out in his life and in his death (i.e., while he was still alive and after his death). There is a doubt if it came out in his life, and there is a doubt if it came out in his death, and there is a doubt if [it came out both] in his life and in his death -- that is "mixed blood." And the Sages say, [if] the doubt occurred in a private domain, it is impure; in a public domain, it is pure. Rather, what is "mixed blood"? One who was killed, from whom a quarter-<i>log</i> of blood in his life and in his death, and it still did not cease [flowing], there is doubt if the majority [came out] in his life and the minority in his death, and a doubt if the majority [came out] in his life and the majority in his death -- behold, that is "mixed blood." Rabbi Yehuda says, one who was killed, who was moved onto a bed, and his blood drips out into a hole -- it is impure, because the drop of death is mixed in with it. And the Sages render it pure, because each first [drop] and every other first [drop] is separated [from the other drops; thus, every new drop is nullified] (see Nidd. 71b:1). Rabbi Shimon says, one who is crucified whose blood streams out, and a quarter-<i>log</i> of blood is found under him -- it is impure. And Rabbi Yehuda renders it pure, for I could say that the last drop had been "standing" on the wood [of the cross]<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote">i.e., the last drop, upon which the soul is dependent and which is therefore impure, remained on the cross itself, while all the blood on the ground had streamed out while he was still alive</i>. In that way, it comes out that you can say, there are three quarter-<i>logs</i>. How so? He received a death-blow, and he was quivering, and a quarter-<i>log</i> of blood came out from him. And they uprooted him [from where he was lying] under the presumption that he was alive, and moved him to another place, and a quarter-<i>log</i> of blood came out from him. [And afterwards they found him dead, and a quarter-<i>log</i> came out of him (GR"A).] The first is the blood of life, the second is the mixed blood, and the third is the blood of death.<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote"><b>ראשונה דם חיים ושניה דם תבוסה ושלישית דם המיתה</b> (GR"A)</i> And what is the [practical] difference between the blood of death and the mixed blood? Rather, that [in regards to the blood of death] the nazir must shave on account of [touching it]; and they are liable on its account for impurity to the Temple and its consecrated objects; [while in regards to] mixed blood, the nazir does not shave on account of it, and they are not liable on its account for impurity to the Temple and its consecrated objects.
Said Rabbi Elazar: Originally, the Elders were divided. Some said, a quarter-<i>log</i> of blood, a quarter-kav of bones, and others said, a half-kav of bones and a half-<i>log</i> of blood. The Beit Din that came after them said, a quarter-<i>log</i> of blood [and] a quarter-kav of bones for terumah and sacrifices, [and] a half-kav of bones and a half-<i>log</i> of blood for the nazir and the Temple. Said Rabbi Elazar: When I went to Damascus, I found Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehuda ben P'tirosh<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote">a.k.a. Rabbi Yehuda ben Beteira (Jastrow)</i>, who were sitting and deliberating about halacha. Yehuda ben P'tirosh says, "A nazir does not shave on account of a quarter-<i>log</i> of blood, and they are not liable on its account for impurity to the Temple and its consecrated objects." Rabbi Meir said to him, "Let this be logically derived from a sheretz. Just as a nazir shaves on account of a sheretz, which is a light matter, and they are liable on its account for impurity to the Temple and its consecrated objects, does it not logically follow that in regards to a quarter-<i>log</i> of blood, which is stringent, a nazir should shave on account of it, and they should be liable on its account for impurity to the Temple and its consecrated objects?" Yehuda ben P'tirosh was silent. I said to Rabbi Meir, "Do not hold me in contempt [for saying this]. Are you familiar with Yehoshua ben Mamal?" He said to me, "Yes, and he was a master of <i>m'lachot</i><sup>*</sup><i class="footnote">i.e., prohibited forms of labor; alt., <i>halachot</i> ("laws") (Tosefta Nazir 5:3)</i>." I said to him, "In the following way did he (i.e., Yehoshua ben Mamal) speak to me in the name of Rabbi Yehoshua: 'Every [form of] impurity from a corpse on account of which the nazir shaves, they are liable on its account for impurity to the Temple and its consecrated objects, and every [form of] impurity from a corpse on account of which the nazir does not shave, they are not liable on its account for impurity to the Temple and its consecrated objects.'"

Chapter 5

Rabbi Yishmael b'Rabbi Yosei says in the name of Rabbi Natan: [In regards to] a tent that is less than four handbreadths, its opening [for purposes of permitting impurity to escape] is its full width and its roof is pure. Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar says in the name of Rabbi Meir, a corpse that is more than four handbreadths, [the requisite size of its] opening is the entire open space. Rabbi Yosei says, the spinal column and the skull are the equivalent of a corpse [for purposes of impurity]. Others say in the name of Rabbi Natan, all the impurities that spread out from a corpse are like a corpse, and its opening is four handbreadths.
A drainpipe that is arched under the house (הבית): If it is four handbreadths wide and has an outlet four handbreadths wide, and [there is] a miscarried fetus inside it, the house remains pure. [If there is] a miscarried fetus in the house, whatever is inside [the drainpipe] remains pure. [If] it has a width of four handbreadths, but its outlet is less than four handbreadths, [and there is] a miscarried fetus inside it, the house is impure. [If there is] a miscarried fetus in the house, whatever is inside [the drainpipe] remains pure. [If] it is less than four handbreadths wide and its outlet is less than four handbreadths wide, [and there is] a miscarried fetus inside it, the house is impure. [If there is] a miscarried fetus inside the house, whatever is inside [the drainpipe] is impure. [If] it is wide from within but narrow from without, [and] the impurity is in the wide part, the house is impure. [If] in the narrow part, it is adjudged half and half. [If] it is impure, whether in the wide part or whether in the narrow part of the house, it is impure inside the house.<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote">Note: The preceding sentence is omitted in MS Vienna.</i> Vessels that are in the wide part are pure, and those that are in the narrow part are impure. Rabbi Yehuda agrees that with regards to crags and crevices, that although they are not similar to a tent, yet they are considered to be [halachically] like a tent (see Mishnah Oholot 3:7).
A cupboard standing either in the house or in the open air: [If] the impurity is inside it, vessels within its thickness (i.e., within niches in the walls of the cupboard) are pure. Impurity that is in its thickness: Vessels that are within (i.e., within the main body of the cupboard but not within the thickness) are pure. And Rabbi Yosei says, it is divided half and half. [If] it was standing inside the house, and the impurity was inside it, the house is impure. [If] the impurity was in the house, whatever is inside [the cupboard] is pure, for the nature of impurity is to go out, and its nature is not to enter. [If] it was girded with ropes, a baby is impure with evening-impurity<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote"><b>טומאת ערב</b> (MS Vienna; GRA)</i>. A plank of reeds that was girded with ropes and covered with plaster, [and] a loaf of bread that was soaked in fruit juices is placed on top of it -- behold, it brings in and protects against [impurity]. Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri says, [as to] all pure cakes, they do not bring in impurity, except for fig-cakes, for they sit (i.e., are stored) in pits underneath [peoples' homes].
A camel that is standing in the airspace [of a source of impurity]: [If] impurity is beneath it, vessels that are on top of it (lit., "on its back") are pure. [If] impurity is on top of it, vessels that are beneath it are pure. [If the camel] was kneeling down, [and] impurity is below it, that which is directly [below] it, until the depths [of the earth], is impure. [If] impurity is on top of it, that which is directly [above] it, until the firmament, is impure. [If] impurity is under its leg or on top of its leg and it kneels down, the impurity breaks forth and ascends, breaks forth and descends. [If] he placed a wooden board above it, [and] impurity is on one side, vessels that are on the other side are impure.
Roof-beams of the house and the attic that are in a line opposite the lower [beams] (see Mishnah Oholot 12:5): [If] impurity is beneath [the lower beams], then [the area below them] is impure; between [the upper and lower beams], between them is impure, but on top of them is pure. [If impurity] is on top of them, [then the area] directly above it up to the firmament is impure, and below them is pure. When do these words apply? When there is a handbreadth's opening between them, [but] if there is not a handbreadth's opening between them, impurity breaks forth and ascends, breaks forth and descends. 
A <i>seah</i>-measure that was laying on its side in the airspace [of a source of impurity] does not bring in impurity underneath its entire [expanse] unless its circumference is four and a half handbreadths (alt., "four handbreadths" (GR"A)). If it was raised half a handbreadth [off the ground], [it does not bring in impurity] unless its circumference is three handbreadths. A column (i.e., which was narrow at top and bottom but bulged in the middle) that was taken into the airspace [of a source of impurity] does not bring in impurity under its side (i.e., under the widest expanse of its bulge) unless its circumference is twenty-four handbreadths. Rabbi Yosei says, twenty-five, for the earth does not absorb (lit., "eat") a handbreadth from them. And so too it says (Jer. 52:23), "And there were ninety-six pomegranates<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote"><b>רמנים</b>, not <b>מנים</b> (accord MS Vienna, GR"A); similarly, Jer. 52:23 has <b>רוחה</b> ("facing outward") not <b>רואה</b> (see following; accord GR"A)</i> facing outward," and then it says (<i>ibid.</i>), "all the pomegranates around the meshwork were one hundred." How is it possible to say "around the meshwork were one hundred" when, behold, it has already said "ninety-six," and how is it possible to say "ninety-six" when it has already said "one hundred"? It was said, from this point forward, four [of the pomegranates] shall be absorbed into the wall, and ninety-six shall be seen from the outside, and its courtyard shall be adjudged like the sloping sides of a tent.
An olive-bulk of a corpse that is stuck in the threshold: Rabbi Eliezer renders the house impure and Rabbi Yehoshua renders it pure (Mishnah Oholot 12:8). [If] it was placed under the threshold, it is adjudged half-and-half for [purposes of] impurity<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote">i.e., if more than half of it is within the threshold, it is considered as though the entire olive's bulk is inside the house</i>, the words of Rabbi Ya'akov. Said Rabbi Shimon, Rabbi Eliezer and Rabbi Yehoshua did not disagree about [an olive-bulk] placed under the threshold, that the house is pure. About what did they disagree? About that which is stuck in the threshold, for Rabbi Eliezer renders the house impure and Rabbi Yehoshua renders it pure. [If] it was stuck in the lintel, the house is impure, and Rabbi Yehoshua (alt., "Rabbi Yosei"; MS Vienna, GR"A) renders it pure, unless it is pushed out by a handbreadth's opening (i.e., the corpse-matter itself forms a tent).
[If there are] two door-projections (<i>zizin</i>)<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote"><b>זיזין</b> ("projection[s] from the door frame serving as a shed over the entrance, or moulding[s] projecting from a window-sill serving as a bracket" (Jastrow)); not <b>חזיזין</b> ("blades of grass"); accord Mishnah Oholot 14:1)</i>, this one on top of that one, and impurity is in the wall that is between them: If even the smallest amount could be seen inside one of them, it is impure, and its fellow is pure, and if not, it is adjudged half-and-half (i.e., the door-projection closest to the impurity is deemed impure, and the other is pure).
Funeral processions that were passing through a public thoroughfare: One's own tent joins together with the tent of the corpse to defile the house, the words of Rabbi Eliezer. And Rabbi Yehoshua says, we do not raise up that which is lower down (i.e., the funeral procession) for [the purpose of] joining tent with tent.

Chapter 16

The mounds that are nearby, between one town and another town, or between roads: Whether they are new or old, they are impure, because women bury their stillborn fetuses there, and lepers bury their limbs there. New [mounds] that are far [from a town or road] are pure; old ones are impure, for I could say, "lest there was once a road or a town there." And what are these "mounds"? These are mounds of dirt that are on top of a cleft [in the earth]. One who erects a tent on top of part of [a mound] is impure and, needless to say, [one who pitches a tent] against all of it. Its dirt is impure on account of being "dirt of a mound." One who dug (חתר) dirt from it, and packed [more dirt] on it, it is not subject to the laws of "dirt of a mound." A mound that was impure and was purified, behold, it is pure, and one need not be concerned lest it revert to impurity.
It is the same whether one finds three corpses, or whether one finds three sepulchers, or whether one finds a sepulcher and a cleft grave and a hastily dug grave [at the same time], or whether one found ten and they do not have between four and eight cubits in between them: They all are subject to [the laws regarding the impurity of the] "surrounding earth," but they are subject to [the laws regarding the impurity of] a "graveyard site" (i.e., a cemetery) (see Mishnah Oholot 16:3), the words of Rabbi Shimon. And the Sages say, one looks at the middle ones as if they were not there, and the outer ones join together from four to eight cubits. [If] he found a [corpse's] head next to its legs, it is not subject to [the laws of] "surrounding earth," and it is not subject to [the laws of] a "graveyard site." The head of this one next to the body of that one, and the head of that one next to the body of this one: They are subject to [the laws of] "surrounding earth," but they are not subject to [the laws of] a "graveyard site." And that which is missing is subject neither to "surrounding earth" nor to a "graveyard site." And what does "missing" mean? Anything that would cause someone to die if it was removed when he was alive. [If] he found two [corpses] for the first time, and one [more] is known [to exist], they are subject to "surrounding earth," but they are not subject to a "graveyard site." It so happened with Rabbi Yeshevav, that he inspected and found two for the first time, and one was known. He made (i.e., designated) for them [an area of] "surrounding earth," [and] he made for them [an area of] a "graveyard site." When he came to the place of Rabbi Akiva, he said, "All your work was in vain. You would have had to inspect every known burial site in the Land of Israel (i.e., according to your logic). They only ruled regarding one who finds three [corpses] for the first time."
[If] one [grave] was found on one side of a public thoroughfare, and two [were found] on the other side, he inspects one side and the other side, and he leaves [a cubit] of the public thoroughfare [unchecked] (see Mishnah Oholot 16:4). Two on one side of a fence, and two on the other side of the fence: He inspects the area of the fence, for it is the [normal] way of a builder to inspect until he reaches the rock (i.e., the rocky substrata), or virgin soil. And what is "virgin soil"?  Anything that does not have clods of fresh-broken ground, and does not have loose earth below it. [If] he inspected and reached water [below the surface], behold, that is virgin soil. [If] he inspected and found clay there, behold, that is like virgin soil. The one who inspects: Beit Shammai say, he checks two [cubits] and leaves one cubit [unchecked], and Beit Hillel say, he checks one cubit and leaves one cubit, the words of Rabbi Yaakov. And the Sages say, Beit Shammai say, he checks two [cubits] and leaves one cubit, and Beit Hillel say, he checks one cubit and leaves two.
One (i.e., a priest) who takes out dirt from an impure place may eat from his <i>dema</i> (i.e., terumah mixed with chullin). Rabbi Yehuda says, he piles this dirt on top of that dirt, but our Rabbis said, the dirt is pure. The one who inspects [the dirt to check for corpse matter] may eat from his <i>dema</i>. The students of Rabban Yochanan ben Zakkai asked him, "The inspector -- what should he eat?" He said to them, "He does not eat." They said to him, "You taught us that he does eat." He said to them, ""Well said. If I have forgotten an act that my own hands performed, and my own eyes witnessed, how much more so one that I have [only] heard with my ears!" And it was not that he did not know; rather, it was that he was trying to stimulate his students. For Rabbi Yehoshua would say, one who teaches and does not exercise [the minds of his students] is like one who sows and does not harvest. One who learns Torah and forgets it is like a woman who bears children and buries them. Rabbi Akiva says, "Review it; continuously review it."
It turns out that one can say that there are three graves: A grave that is discovered: They may remove it, [but once] it is removed, the area is impure, and forbidden to derive benefit from. A grave which endangers the public: They may remove it, [and once] it is removed, the area is pure and permitted to derive benefit from. A grave which is known: They may not remove it, [but] if they did remove it, they area is pure and permitted to derive benefit from. A field in which a grave was lost (i.e., its location was forgotten) is pure, [but] one who enters [the field] is impure. [If] he examined [the field] and found a grave inside it: One who enters [the field] is pure, for I could reason, "The grave that was lost is the grave that was found." [If] he examined and found three graves inside it: One who enters [the field] is pure, and one need not be concerned lest they are subject to [the laws of] a "graveyard site" (i.e., a cemetery) (see Mishnah Oholot 16:3). One who removes his grave (i.e., a grave on his property) to the public thoroughfare, and a person was walking there, he removes one bone [at a time], and everything is pure. One who dug out his grave inside his field, they say to him, "Collect it bone by bone, and everything is pure." It so happened with the brothers<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote"><b>אחין</b>, not <b>אחיו</b> ("his brothers") (Zuckermandel)</i> Yehuda and Hillel, the sons of Rabban Gamaliel, that they were walking on the border of Oney, [and] they found a certain man who had dug up his grave inside of his field. They said to him, "Collect it bone by bone, and everything is pure."
One whose burial is unauthorized is not [considered] part of a "graveyard site" and is not subject to [the laws of] "the surrounding earth of the grave." Rabban Shimon ben Gamaliel says, a stillborn does not acquire a grave and is not subject to "surrounding earth," and one whose burial is unauthorized is subject to "surrounding earth." Rabbi Yehuda says, a pit into which stillborn are dropped is pure. Said Rabbi Yehuda, it so happened with a certain [Roman] tax collector in Rimon, that he dropped a stillborn into a pit, and a certain priest came with his front plate (<i>tzitz</i>) to determine what was dropped [into the pit], and that incident was brought before [the Sages] and they rendered it pure, because weasels and hyenas can be found there, and [it is presumed that] they would drag [the stillborn] from there (see Mishnah Oholot 18:8).
A grave that was sealed: [If] he opened it, it does not import impurity in any of the surrounding area. [If] he broke open its boards, and went back and sealed it, it imparts impurity on its surrounding area. [If] he made a breach in it, one breach of four handbreadths, whether above or below, it only imparts impurity opposite the breach. [If] he had opened one aperture to the north, and opened another aperture to the south, the south purifies the north.

There is a stringency with the land of the nations that there is not with a <i>beit haperas</i>, and with a <i>beit haperas</i> that there is not with the land of the nations: That with the land of the nations, virgin [soil] that has not been defiled, and then becomes defiled when one enters it, and it cannot be purified from its defilement, which is not the case with a <i>beit haperas</i>. The stringency with a <i>beit haperas</i> is that even if the four sides surrounding it are pure, behold, it still has the presumption of a <i>beit haperas</i>. The land of the nations: If one is able to enter it in a state of purity, it is pure. And how close must one be to be able to enter it in a state of purity? Rabban Shimon ben Gamaliel says, even one furrow, behold, that interrupts. Said Rabbi Shimon, I could feed priests foods prepared in purity in a tannery that is in Tzidon (i.e., north of the Land of Israel), or that is in the towns that are in Lebanon, because they are adjacent to the sea or to the river. They said to him, behold, the idols interrupt. § The presumption of the roads of those who come up from Babylonia: Even though they are absorbed within the land of the nations, they are pure. Rabban Shimon ben Gamaliel says, up until a place that a person can turn to the right and the left without embarrassment. Towns that have been absorbed within the Land of Israel, such as Susita and its neighbors, [and] Ashkelon and its neighbors, even though they are exempt from tithes and from the [laws of] the sabbatical year, they are not considered [part of] the land of the nations.
One who enters the land of the nations in chest or in a cupboard is pure. In a coach, in a wagon, or in a boat, or on a raft, he is impure. One who inserts his head and the majority of his body into the land of the nations is impure. An earthenware vessel that entered the airspace of the land of the nations is impure. Benches and <i>cathedras</i> (a cushioned chair with a back) whose majority entered the land of the nations are impure.
Everyone can convert [an Israelite dwelling] into a "dwelling places of the nations," even a slave, even a maidservant, and even a eunuch, and even a minor of nine years and one day. <sup>*</sup><i class="footnote">The following sentence ("An idolater...") follows the GR"A, as the printed text is corrupt.</i>An idolater that is married, and a Samaritan that is married, an idolater whose slaves are Samaritans, and a Samaritan whose slaves are idolaters, make a "dwelling place of the nations." Regarding "suspending" the laws of dwelling places of the nations (i.e., in connection with eating terumah and consecrated foods), Rabbi Yosei b'Rabbi Yehuda says, they only burn it. And how long must [a Gentile] stay in [a dwelling] in order that it requires inspection? Aba Yudan a man of Tzidon in the name of Rabbi Eliezer [says], forty days, even if he does not have a wife, and the halacha is according to his words. [If] this one was entering and that one was leaving, and [neither] remained there for forty days, it is pure, and if [one of them] remained there for forty days, it is impure with corpse-impurity.<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote">After forty days, a Gentile is suspected of having buried a dead fetus under the ground of a dwelling (Rambam).</i> A [Gentile] watchman: [If] he remains there for forty days [without leaving], it is impure. When he is one that [is permitted] to come and go, even though he remains there forty days, it is pure because he has the presumption of one who watches<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote">per the GR"A, since he has come and gone during those forty days, rendering his dwelling impermanent, the Israelite's residence is not conferred the status of a dwelling place of a Gentile</i>. Everyone is trusted to watch [the dwelling], and even a slave, and even a woman, and even a eunuch, and even a minor of nine years and one day.
Sewer-pipes that travel from the land of the nations into the Land of Israel: He makes two<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote">"two" is omitted in MS Vienna</i> grates for them and he reduces [their circumference] until it is less than a handbreadth.
How do they inspect a dwelling place of the nations (Mishnah Oholot 18:8). An Israelite enters first, and afterwards a priest. Even if they found that [the dwelling place] was inlaid with alabaster or with mosaic (פספס) (see GR"A), it is impure.
A store is not subject to the laws of "dwelling places of the nations" (Mishnah Oholot 18:10). Even if it was destroyed, behold, it is impure and is not [subject to the laws of] dwelling places of the nations.<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote">alt., "A store is not subject to the laws of "dwelling places of the nations" unless [a non-Jew] resides within it. A colonnade is not subject to the laws of "dwelling places of the nations unless [a non-Jew] resides within it. [Regarding] a dwelling-place of the nations, even if it was destroyed, etc." (MS Vienna, GR"A)</i> And a <i>beit haperas</i> outside the Land [of Israel], horse-mangers<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote"><b>הריות</b> (GR"A)</i>, and workshops, and public baths, and a gate-house, and a reservoir, and the open spaces of the courtyard, and fruit-shelters<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote"><b>הברגנין</b> (GR"A; see Mishnah Oholot 18:10)</i>, and the way stations, and the temporary huts (succot), the field-huts, and the tents, are not subject to the laws of dwelling places of the nations. Armories and wineries [of gentiles]: Rabbi Yehuda renders them impure and Rabbi Yosei renders them pure<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote">The Mishnah (Mishnah Oholot 18:10) holds like Rabbi Yosei in regards to armories, but does not reference wineries</i>. A gate-house and the open spaces of the courtyard: At a time that they are intermingled with dwelling places of the nations, they are impure on account of the laws of dwelling places of the nations. And double-rows of colonnades: Even if they become intermingled with dwelling places of the nations, they are not subject to the laws of dwelling-places of the nations.
And what is the "east of Caesarea" (Mishnah Oholot 18:9)? From opposite its <i>titraphlon</i><sup>*</sup><i class="footnote">lit., "mansion-house," the name of a prominent building in Caesarea (Jastrow)</i>, until opposite its wine press. Yehuda the Baker testified about the northeast<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote"><b>ספונו המזרחי</b>; alt., <b>םטיו המזרחי</b> ("the eastern colonnade") (MS Vienna)</i>, that it is [considered] pure, and the remainder of all of it is impure on account of [being part of] the land of the nations.
One who travels from Akko to Cheziv in our days: The road is [considered] pure on account of being [part of] the land of the nations, and it is liable in tithes and in [the laws of] the sabbatical year, unless it is known [with certainty] that it is exempt. From the left-hand side to the west of the road is impure on account of being the land of the nations, and exempt from tithes and from [the laws of] the sabbatical year, unless it is known [with certainty] that it is liable, until Cheziv. Rabbi Yishmael b'Rabbi Yosei says in the name of his father, until Laclabi<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote">alt., "Lablabo" (Jastrow)</i>.
Originally, they would say that the borders [of Ashkelon (MS Vienna)], from Kever HaGadol<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote">lit., "the large grave"; per Zar Zahav, this is a place name</i> until the Negev, and until Tar'in, were impure. And the Sages voted on them (i.e., the borders), and rendered them pure. Rabbi Yehuda ben Yaakov from Beit Guvria, and Yaakov b'Rabbi Yitzchak from Beit Gufnin, testified about Caesarea, that [the Israelites] had always had it in their possession, but that the Sages permitted it, that it no longer be counted [as part of the Land of Israel<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote">i.e., for purposes of tithing and impurity</i>]. Said Rabbi Chanin, that same year was the sabbatical year, and the idolaters went to their circuses, and they left the marketplace full of produce, and the Israelites came and plundered it. Upon their return, they said, "Come, let us go to the Sages, lest they permit pigs to them." Said Rabbi Zerika, on the fifth day of the second Adar, twenty-four elders voted on it and they permitted it, for everyone was entering inside it.
It so happened with Rebbi and Rabbi Yishmael b'Rabbi Yosei and Rabbi Eliezer HaKappar (Bar Kappara), that they spent the Sabbath in the store of Pazi<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote"><b>פזי</b> not <b>עובד כוכבים</b> ("an idolater") (MS Vienna, Zar Zahav); <b>פזי</b> was likely misread as <i>גוי</i> ("non-Jew") and then altered to "idolater" due to censorship; see also Tosefta Yevamot 14:11, where a similar phenomenon occurs with <b>גייס</b></i> in Lod, and Rabbi Pinchas ben Yair was sitting before them. They said to him, "Ashkelon, what is your (pl.) ruling about it (i.e., whether it is considered a dwelling place of the nations)?" He said to them, "They sell wheat in their <i>basilicas</i><sup>*</sup><i class="footnote"><b>בסילקאות</b> ("building with colonnades for holding courts, also meeting place for merchants, exchange, forum") (Jastrow)</i>, and they join<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote"><b>וטופלין</b>; alt., <b>ותובלין ואוכלין</b> ("and they immerse themselves and eat") (GR"A)</i> their Pesach offerings in the evening." They said to him, "What is the ruling for one who traveled in it from the land of the nations?" He said to them, "Once he lingers in it forty days." They said to him, "In that case, come (pl.) and we will vote on it, whether to exempt it from tithes or not." Rabbi Yishmael b'Rabbi Yosei did not vote with them. When he removed [himself], he<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote">per the Yerushalmi (Yer. Yevamot VII:3.4), "he" is  Bar Kappara</i> said, "Rabbi, for what reason are you not voting with us?" He said to him, "Concerning one impurity that I [previously] rendered impure, I [have] rendered it pure. [But] not tithes. I am afraid of the Supreme Sanhedrin, lest they cut off my head!"
