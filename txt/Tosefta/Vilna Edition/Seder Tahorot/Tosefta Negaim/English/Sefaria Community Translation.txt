Chapter 6

A leprous house (Lev. 14:33) has never existed and never will exist. Why, then, is it written about? Only to say, "Expound it and receive your reward." [However,] Rabbi Elazar son of Rabbi Shimon says, there was a place in the area of Gaza, and they would call it a "leprous ruin"<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote"><i>churvata s'girta</i> (specifically, a ruin declared leprous after its period of quarantine)</i>. Rabbi Shimon ben Yehuda says in the name of<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote"><b>משום</b> (GR"A)</i> a man from K'far Akko, there was a place in the Galilee, and they would mark it, for they would would say that there were leprous stones in it. And Jerusalem was never contaminated with leprosy. Said Rabbi Yehuda, I never heard that [about all of Jerusalem]; rather, only about the Holy Temple.

Chapter 8



Said Rabbi Yehuda, it was my Shabbat [to serve], and I went to the place of Rabbi Tarfon. In his house, he said to me, "Yehuda, by son, give me my sandal," and I gave it to him. He stretched his hand out to the window, and handed me a staff.<sup>*</sup><i class="footnote">Translation follows MS Vienna, GR"A.</i> He said to me, "My son, with this I purified three lepers." And I learned seven laws [from this]: That it is made of a cypress branch; and its head is covered with leaves; that its length is a cubit; and its thickness is a quarter of the leg of of bedstead, cut in two and then in four; that it can be used for sprinkling twice or three times; that it may purify [when the leper is] inside the house and when not inside the house; and that it may be used for purification in the outlying areas (i.e., outside Jerusalem).
